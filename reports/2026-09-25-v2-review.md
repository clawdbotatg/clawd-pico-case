# V2 review and next-version goals

2026-09-25. Source: Austin's hands-on report of the V2 print.
Design: `prototype-v2`, commit `f278436`, internal CAD revision R5.
Original first-principles MIT case; no third-party case geometry used.

**V2 failed the physical fit test. Do not reprint it unchanged.**
This document records results and a correction brief, not a new design.

## What V2 tried to do

Keep the existing case and add five requested changes: an internally
retained ball joystick, pry access, corrected USB-C opening, wider/taller
rectangular buttons, and bottom access to the pink board's button.

The joystick used a smaller ball, shaft and internal flange. To clear the
flange's assumed movement, the design raised the lid locally around the
joystick: the collar top was 3.4 mm above the original flat lid surface.
It did not raise the entire lid, but it changed the exterior and printing
surface. With the lid printed upside down, the collar held the main face
above the bed, requiring supports beneath it. The joystick also required
support review under its flange/ball.

That was an unnecessary complication relative to Austin's requested change.
The designer should have preserved the flat lid and proven joystick fit,
and treated support-free printing as a design constraint.

## What worked — reported by Austin

- Pry ledge/notch makes the case easier to open. Keep it.
- Bottom access hole lines up with the board button. Keep its location.
- Button caps are more rectangular as requested. Keep that improvement.
- The earlier joystick fitted the PCB joystick and moved correctly.
  Use that successful interface and movement clearance as the baseline.

## What failed — reported by Austin

- V2 joystick does not fit onto the PCB joystick.
- The raised joystick area and supports introduced unwanted complexity.
- The extra removable printed layer/supports caused problems, including
  damage or obstruction at the button openings.

The exact cause of the socket misfit is not established. Do not assume it
is simply socket width: orientation, support residue, engagement depth,
stem lip and actual exported geometry need comparison with the working cap.
The exact slicer construction of the peel-off layer has not been inspected;
the design's need for supports is known, but calling that layer a raft or
a particular support interface would be a guess.

USB alignment, taller-button feel, and long-term retention/durability have
not received a specific successful fit report. Rectangular shape working
does not mean the damaged button openings or all button operation passed.

## Next-version requirements

1. Restore the original flat lid. No raised joystick collar or case height
   increase to accommodate it.
2. Recover the exact earlier joystick geometry that fitted and moved well.
   Preserve its PCB socket/contact and movement clearance. The successful
   physical cap's source revision must be matched; do not silently assume
   the most recent older CAD was the one Austin tested.
3. Add a small retaining lip **inside** the case. Cap goes onto the PCB
   joystick first, then lid goes over it. Lip must retain without binding.
4. Replace the flat thumb disc with a smaller, taller rounded ball that
   passes through the lid opening during assembly. Lip cannot pass through.
5. Design for printing without the added support layer or supports that
   damage the lid and button holes. Confirm this in sliced toolpaths before
   another full print; do not solve geometry problems by adding supports.
6. Preserve the successful pry feature, bottom access position and
   rectangular buttons. Keep unrelated changes to a minimum.
7. Keep original MIT geometry, measurement provenance and every iteration
   in GitHub. Never overwrite the failed V2 tag or its results.

## Verification before another full case

- Compare working and failed joystick interfaces, including print orientation.
- Test the revised joystick on the actual PCB: seat, tilt, centre click,
  tilted click and free return before and after fitting the flat lid.
- Check lip capture and installation path without increasing case height.
- Inspect slicing: flat lid contact, clean through-holes, no added support
  layer. A small joystick/lid test piece is preferable before a full case.
- Keep physical checks separate from CAD checks. V2 passed 276 geometry
  checks yet failed in use; those checks did not establish socket fit,
  support removal quality or actual joystick movement.

## Handoff

No CAD changes, slicing or printer requests are authorized by this review
alone. Austin asked for this document first. Either agent can continue after
direction; a fresh review is useful, but must follow CLAUDE.md and stay away
from third-party case files. Start with this brief and the working cap,
not with further modifications to V2's raised collar.
